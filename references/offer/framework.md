# LadderFunnel Offer Stack Framework

The goal of the Offer Stack is to replace a free trial with a sequence of micro-commitments that liquidate ad spend on Day 1, while simultaneously creating intense desire for the core SaaS product.

Default recommendation: use the standard 3-step sequence below.

Alternative variant: it is also valid to run the intro product at `$27` with **no order bump** and then transition directly into the upsell. This simpler structure can work well when the intro offer is more tangible or outcome-driven, but it usually requires more trust, stronger positioning, and more persuasive copy than the classic `$7 + bump` front-end.

If the user does not explicitly want the simpler variant, default to the classic 3-step structure:

## 1. The Intro Product ($7)
This is not a "lite" version of the SaaS. It is an information/educational product (video training, playbook, templates, or blueprint).
*   **The Psychology:** The Intro Product teaches the customer *how* to solve their problem manually or strategically. By showing them the manual process, they realize how painful it is to do without software.
*   **The Positioning:** It must solve an immediate, acute pain point.
*   **The Price:** Always $7. This filters out freebie-seekers and acquires a "buyer."

*When a user asks you to generate an Intro Product idea, ask them: "What is the painful manual process that your SaaS automates?"*
*Then, output 3 ideas for a $7 video training or playbook that teaches that manual process.*

## 2. The Order Bump ($27)
This is a one-click add-on presented on the checkout page.
*   **The Psychology:** It speeds up or makes the $7 Intro Product easier. It *complements* the intro product; it does not introduce a new concept.
*   **The Format:** Templates, swipe files, checklists, resource lists, or scripts. 
*   **The Price:** $27. We expect a 40-50% take rate.

*When generating an order bump, ensure it is a tangible asset (like "The 50 High-Converting Email Templates") that directly makes the $7 training faster to implement.*

## 3. The Core SaaS Upsell (Recurring)
This is presented immediately *after* the customer purchases the $7 product and the $27 bump, before the thank you page.
*   **The Psychology:** "You just bought the blueprint and the templates. Now, you *could* do it all manually... or you can just use our software which automates the entire process in one click."
*   **The Transition:** The SaaS is the ultimate "easy button" for what they just bought.
*   **The Offer:** Usually a discounted first month (e.g., $97/mo, but get the first month for $47) or an exclusive annual deal.

*When generating the upsell, clearly articulate how the SaaS automates the exact manual strategy they just purchased in the $7 intro product.*

## Alternative Front-End: $27 Intro Product, No Bump

Use this only when the user explicitly wants a cleaner 2-step funnel or when the intro product is strong enough to carry a higher front-end price.

*   **The Structure:** `$27 intro product -> recurring upsell`
*   **The Tradeoff:** Higher average order value on the front end, but the page usually needs more trust, more specificity, and more persuasive depth to convert cold traffic.
*   **When it fits:** Strong niche pain, founder-led trust, tangible implementation training, or a clearly productized "DIY blueprint" offer.
*   **What to avoid:** Do not casually recommend this as the default if the user has weak proof, broad messaging, or low market trust.

When using this variant:

*   The intro product should feel more complete and concrete than a lightweight `$7` playbook.
*   The copy should work harder to justify the higher entry price.
*   The upsell should still be positioned as the speed, automation, or done-for-you version of the exact manual process taught in the intro product.
